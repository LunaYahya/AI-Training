## Key Concepts
- Bivariate Analysis: Analyzing the relation between two variables to uncover patterns, trends, or possible relationships. Scatter plot is used to visualize the relationship between two numerical variables, and a grouped box plot is used to visualize the distribution of a numerical variable among groups of data.
- Correlation: This metric measures the relationship between two numerical variables, showing whether the relationship is positive, negative, or neutral and how strong it is. It ranges from -1 (perfect negative relationship) through 0 (no linear relationship), and +1 (perfect positive relationship).
- Correlation Heatmap: This visual representation of the correlation matrix allows easily identifying pairs of highly positively or negatively correlated numerical features.
- Correlation Is Not Causation: Despite the fact that two variables may be strongly correlated, it does not imply that they have a causal relationship; the correlation simply shows that there is movement in both variables.
- Pair Plot: This visualization tool contains scatter plots for all pairs of numerical variables, and histograms of each variable distribution, placed on the diagonal.
- Data Storytelling: The act of converting data analysis into a coherent story by detailing the key patterns, relations, and insights that have been identified during EDA and the implications thereof for future ML projects.
